What changed this month

October 2026
  • RBA

    The RBA raised the cash rate 25 basis points to 4.85% on 3 November, its fifth increase of 2026 and the highest setting in 18 years, on a six to three vote. Markets had priced an 88% chance of the move after the September quarter trimmed mean came in above the Bank's forecast, and Deputy Governor Hauser had said on 21 October that the inflation outlook looked materially worse than projected in August. After the decision, markets priced about an even chance of a further rise taking the cash rate above 5%. The decision falls after this edition's 31 October data date and is included because it was announced on publication day.

  • Inflation

    Headline CPI rose to 4.3% in the year to September from 4.0% in August. Electricity rose 12.8% over the year as Commonwealth rebates ended and automotive fuel 23.6%, including 9.4% in the month, while housing at 5.2% was the largest contributor. The monthly trimmed mean rose to 3.6% from a revised 3.5%. In the September quarter, headline CPI rose 1.3% (3.9% over the year) and the trimmed mean 1.0% against the RBA's August forecast of 0.83%, lifting the annual rate to 3.7%. Melbourne Institute consumer inflation expectations rose to 5.3% in October, the highest since June.

  • Jobs and business

    Unemployment rose to 4.7% in September from 4.6% as employment fell 39,000 and participation eased 0.2 points to 66.8%; trend unemployment is 4.6%. NAB business confidence fell 4 points to minus 13 in September, its third consecutive fall, while conditions held at minus 1 and the employment index fell to its lowest in five years. Westpac Melbourne Institute consumer sentiment fell a further 4.7% to 80.4 in October.

  • Central banks

    The Federal Reserve held at 3.75% to 4.00% on 28 October on a 10 to 2 vote, with Cook and Hammack voting for a rise, and futures priced about 85% for an increase on 9 December. The ECB held its deposit rate at 2.50% on 29 October, and the Bank of Japan held at 1.25% on 30 October on an 8 to 1 vote with Takata proposing 1.50%. The Bank of Canada raised 25 basis points to 2.50% on 28 October, its first increase since July 2023, and the RBNZ held at 2.75% on a 4 to 3 vote when markets had expected a rise.

  • Bond yields

    Bonds rallied as equities fell. The United States 10 year closed October at about 4.92%, down 36 basis points, after a close of 5.31% on 5 October, and the Australian 10 year at about 5.12%, down 23 basis points, after trading near 5.40% on 1 October. The Bloomberg AusBond Composite returned 1.2% and the Global Aggregate hedged 1.5%. Global government bonds outperformed credit, and the ICE BofA United States high yield spread widened 32 basis points to 344.

  • Technology and credit

    Doubts about the return on AI spending hit semiconductors, Asian technology and AI-linked credit from mid-month. The Nasdaq fell 5.3%, its worst month since March 2025, the KOSPI fell 16.2% in local currency and MSCI Emerging Markets 9.1% in Australian dollars. CoreWeave's five year credit default swap spread rose above 1,100 basis points. In Australia, Firmus withdrew its ASX float on 9 October and NextDC fell 41.7% on 21 October after cutting FY27 revenue guidance.

  • Housing

    Cotality's national index fell 1.2% in October, a seventh consecutive monthly decline, leaving values 6.7% below the March peak and 2.8% lower over twelve months. Brisbane fell 2.0%, Perth 1.7% and Sydney 1.6%, with Sydney now 9.4% lower over the year. The combined capitals are down 8.1% over seven months, which Cotality describes as the fastest downturn in forty years. Dwelling approvals rose 5.1% in September on units.

  • Oil

    Brent finished October at US$117.6 on the expiring December contract, up 13.6% for the month and 93.3% for the calendar year, with the January contract at US$113.4, as Middle East supply stayed disrupted. Morgan Stanley left its December quarter 2026 forecast at US$105 and raised its December quarter 2027 forecast to US$90. Fuel added 9.4% to Australian prices in September alone.

  • Australian equities

    The ASX 200 Accumulation Index returned 1.4% in October to close at 8,908 and is up 5.2% for the calendar year. Utilities (+9.3%), financials (+6.9%) and health care (+6.3%) led as investors moved out of resources, while materials fell 7.8% and information technology 3.4%. A-REITs returned 1.7% and small caps minus 6.4%. NextDC's 41.7% fall on 21 October was its worst day since listing.

  • Global equities

    The MSCI All Countries World Index returned minus 2.8% in Australian dollars, with the currency up 1.2% to 0.703. MSCI World ex Australia returned minus 1.9% unhedged and minus 0.7% hedged. In local currency the S&P 500 returned minus 0.9% while the equal weight index rose nearly 5%, and TOPIX returned minus 4.7% in Australian dollars. With 60% of the S&P 500 reported, September quarter earnings were up 30.2% and 82% of companies beat estimates, by an average of 1.9%.

Overall signal: expansion. Eleven of twelve variables green with job sentiment the sole recession reading, and no indicator changes in September. ClearBridge frames the Federal Reserve's 16 September rise as the start of a cycle entered from strength, with consumer credit growing at the weakest non-recessionary pace on record and most borrowing costs still below their 2023 peaks. The Bankrate 30 year fixed mortgage rate is 7.3% against 8.1% in 2023, investment grade corporate bonds yield about 6.0% against 6.4%, and high yield bonds 8.4% against 9.5%. Federal funds futures price roughly 115 basis points of cumulative hikes over just over a year, a steeper path than the Federal Reserve's own projections. ClearBridge notes that would still be the smallest tightening cycle in modern history, against 425 basis points in 2004 to 2006 and 525 basis points in 2022 to 2023. Consensus has S&P 500 earnings per share growing 18.7% over the next twelve months.

ClearBridge had not published an October update at the time of writing, so this reading is as at 30 September. Since then the Federal Reserve has held with two dissents in favour of a rise, the advance estimate put United States September quarter GDP growth at 3.4% annualised, up from 2.2%, and the 10 year Treasury yield fell to about 4.92%. The ICE BofA United States high yield spread widened 32 basis points in October to 344, its widest close of the month on 30 October, which is the indicator to watch against ClearBridge's green credit spreads reading.

Risk register

ElevatedBond yields and the discount rate

Yields eased in October from multi-year highs. The United States 10 year closed at about 4.92% after 5.31% on 5 October, and the Australian 10 year at about 5.12% after trading near 5.40% on 1 October, still 0.76 and 0.38 points above their levels at the start of the year. Futures priced about 85% for a Federal Reserve rise on 9 December, and the RBA raised to 4.85% on 3 November with markets pricing about an even chance of a further rise above 5%.

ElevatedEnergy and the Strait of Hormuz

Brent finished October at US$117.6 on the expiring December contract, up 93.3% for the calendar year, as Middle East supply remained disrupted. Automotive fuel rose 23.6% over the year to September and carried Australian headline CPI to 4.3%. Morgan Stanley holds its December quarter 2026 Brent forecast at US$105.

ElevatedAustralian housing correction

Values fell 1.2% in October, a seventh consecutive fall, and sit 6.7% below the March peak and 2.8% lower over twelve months. Cotality describes the 8.1% fall in combined capital values over seven months as the fastest downturn in forty years, and the 3 November rise to 4.85% adds to repayment pressure on variable rate borrowers.

ElevatedPrivate credit and AI-linked borrowing

ASIC's concerns remain centred on development and construction lending after the Bathla administration and the suspension of Metrics wholesale fund redemptions on 30 September. In October stress appeared in AI-linked credit: CoreWeave's five year credit default swap spread rose above 1,100 basis points, and the ICE BofA United States high yield spread widened 32 basis points to 344.

ElevatedBusiness margins and the labour market

Unemployment rose to 4.7% in September as employment fell 39,000, above the RBA's August peak of 4.6%. NAB business confidence fell to minus 13, its third consecutive fall, and the survey's employment index fell to its lowest in five years, with profitability at minus 5. Raised from Watch this month on the fall in employment.

UpsideUnited States earnings delivery

With 60% of the S&P 500 reported, September quarter earnings grew 30.2% and 82% of companies beat estimates, and the advance estimate put United States growth at 3.4% annualised. The beats were the smallest since late 2022 at 1.9%, and the Nasdaq fell 5.3% in October as investors questioned returns on AI spending.

Structural: the productivity gap

Ausbil, Jim Chronis · 7 May 2026 · updated twice yearly

Australian productivity has been flat at 0% since 2022 while United States productivity has run at 2% or better for five years, a gap Chronis illustrated as the difference between travelling at 60 and 100 kilometres an hour. Unit labour costs have grown around 5% annually over the past two years against 2% in the United States, which makes the inflation target structurally harder for the RBA to reach and sustains restrictive policy independently of where the cycle sits.

Government spending sits at roughly 28% of GDP with investment at only 5%, maintaining the existing capital stock rather than adding productive capacity, while consumption including the NDIS continues to grow and crowd it out. Ausbil expects GDP to slow from 2.3% to around 1.8% and remain below trend until a private sector led pickup in the second half of 2027, which Chronis characterises as a moderation rather than a contraction. The 2025-26 financial year came in at 2.4%, ahead of that path, and this section is next due for refresh in November.
