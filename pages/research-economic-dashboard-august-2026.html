House view

written 7 Aug 2026

Global growth slowed through the first half of 2026 with the June quarter the likely low point, and both NAB (3.0% for calendar 2026) and Ausbil (3.3%) expect a recovery from the September quarter, conditional on Middle East hostilities not persisting.

Inflation remains above target in Australia at 3.6% trimmed mean and in the United States at 3.4% core PCE, and in both cases the policy risk is skewed towards further tightening rather than easing, with NAB not expecting an RBA cut until the June quarter of 2027.

A cash rate held at 4.35% for an extended period supports keeping duration short of benchmark and retaining the floating rate credit exposure added through the Realm positions, while the ASX 200 at 17.5 times forward earnings against a long-run 14.9 leaves the domestic market carrying valuation risk into a reporting season where consensus is already falling.

What changed this month

July 2026
  • Oil

    Brent rose 23.6% over the month to US$90.10 a barrel on the renewal of US-Iran hostilities, reversing the decline that followed the June ceasefire. NAB now describes risks to its US$80 year-end assumption as tilted to the upside, and flags inventory drawdowns as the binding constraint if Gulf infrastructure takes further damage.

  • Global growth

    NAB cut 2026 global GDP growth to 3.0% from 3.1%, attributing the revision to weaker than expected Chinese June quarter GDP and a fall in Russian March quarter GDP, while lifting 2027 to 3.3% on expected Chinese policy support.

  • Labour market

    Australian employment rose 76,300 in June with the unemployment rate at 4.4%, which broke the 4.1% to 4.3% range that had held for six months. Sally Auld reads the rate as drifting higher from here and has flagged the RBA's own 4.6% cycle peak as possibly too low, which she treats as the signal that tightening is finished.

  • China

    June quarter GDP grew 4.3% year on year, the weakest outcome since 2022 and below the government's 4.5% to 5.0% target, with domestic demand soft and growth carried by exports and industrial production. Hi-tech production rose 14.1% year on year in June.

  • Australian equities

    The ASX 200 gained 2.3% to 8,976.8 and led global peers in both local currency and USD terms, with Financials contributing 196bps of the 226bps index return and CBA alone contributing 81.5bps. Small Ordinaries fell 3.2% over the same month.

  • Earnings

    Consensus FY26 aggregate EPS growth was revised down 1.0 percentage point to 11.9% and FY27 down 1.7 points to 11.3%, with the downgrades concentrated in Energy and Materials. Morgan Stanley flags Banks, housing-related names and consumer-facing sectors as carrying the most downside risk into August results.

Structural: the productivity gap

Ausbil, Jim Chronis · 7 May 2026 · updated twice yearly

Australian productivity has been flat at 0% since 2022 while United States productivity has run at 2% or better for five years, a gap Chronis illustrated as the difference between travelling at 60 and 100 kilometres an hour. Unit labour costs have grown around 5% annually over the past two years against 2% in the United States, which makes the inflation target structurally harder for the RBA to reach and sustains restrictive policy independently of where the cycle sits.

Government spending sits at roughly 28% of GDP with investment at only 5%, maintaining the existing capital stock rather than adding productive capacity, while consumption including the NDIS continues to grow and crowd it out. Ausbil expects GDP to slow from 2.3% to around 1.8% and remain below trend until a private sector led pickup in the second half of 2027, which Chronis characterises as a moderation rather than a contraction.
