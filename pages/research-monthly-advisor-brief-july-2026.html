Key market events in July
23 July
The Gulf ceasefire collapses and oil reverses June's fall

June's peace framework broke down as Washington and Tehran resumed strikes, Yemen's Houthis declared a maritime blockade against Saudi Arabia and Saudi forces joined US operations against Iran-backed groups in Iraq. Brent crude topped US$100/bbl intraday on 23 July and finished the month up 20.4%, reversing June's 20.9% fall almost exactly, while the S&P GSCI rose 10.7%. Saudi Arabia convened representatives of 43 countries in the final week to discuss a maritime coalition protecting shipping routes.

28 July
A reported Chinese lithography breakthrough triggers a global chip rout

Reports that China had begun mass producing deep ultraviolet lithography tools, a technology ASML has long monopolised, prompted an immediate reassessment of the AI semiconductor trade. Korea's Kospi fell 10.8% on the day to 6,023.66 and triggered circuit breakers, with Samsung Electronics down 13.4% and SK Hynix down 14.7%; the two names carry roughly half the index. Chinese memory maker CXMT listed in Shanghai on 27 July and rose 466% on debut.

29 July
Warsh's second FOMC leaves the long end unconvinced

The Fed held at 3.50% to 3.75% for a fifth consecutive meeting on a 9 to 3 vote, with all three dissents favouring a 25 basis point hike, and Warsh declined to offer forward guidance while stating there is no soft inflation target. Markets cut the odds of a September hike from 82% on 23 July to around 63% by month end, while simultaneously selling the long end: the 30-year yield rose more than 9 basis points on the day to close July at 5.25%, its highest since 2007. Eric Winograd of AllianceBernstein described the press conference to clients as confusing and often internally contradictory.

30 July
A record US reporting season halts the selloff in its final week

With 61% of the S&P 500 reported, 86% had beaten EPS estimates against a five-year average of 78%, the highest beat rate since the second quarter of 2021, on blended revenue growth of 14.1% and the strongest earnings growth since 2021. Microsoft rose 15.5% on 30 July, its best single day in close to eighteen years, and Amazon added 15.3% the following session on cloud results. Those two prints did more to stabilise global semiconductors than any policy response: the Kospi rose 17.91% on 31 July, its largest single-day gain on record.

Macro

What moved markets in July

July reversed June on almost every axis, then reversed itself in the final two sessions. Renewed conflict in the Gulf lifted Brent 20.4% and rotated capital into energy, a reported Chinese lithography breakthrough ended the AI semiconductor trade's twelve-month run, and a second FOMC meeting under Kevin Warsh produced a bear steepening as the long end lost confidence in the Fed's inflation commitment. Record US earnings arriving in the last week stopped the rout: Korea's Kospi finished 22.2% lower and Japan's Nikkei 225 8.1% lower in local currency, both after outsized final-session rallies. The S&P/ASX 200 Accumulation Index rose 2.3%, a fourth consecutive monthly gain, against a fall of 1.6% for the MSCI All Countries World ex-Australia Index in Australian dollars.

Equities

ASX 200 Accumulation +2.3% and MSCI ACWI ex-Australia (AUD) -1.6%. In local currency the Kospi fell 22.2%, the Nikkei 225 8.1% and the S&P 500 0.1%.

Fed September hike odds moved from below 53% mid-month to 82% on 23 July to roughly 63% at month end. RBA year-end hike odds fell from 60% to effectively nil.

Credit

AusBond Credit fell 0.1% and Credit FRN rose 0.4%, while Global Corporate Credit hedged fell 1.2% as the US 30-year closed at 5.25%. Global High Yield was flat.

Commodities

Brent crude rose 20.4% and the S&P GSCI 10.7%. Gold added 1.0% and thermal coal 0.4%, while Dalian iron ore fell 4.8%.

Inflation & Policy

June quarter trimmed mean CPI rose 0.8% to 3.6% annually, below the RBA's own 3.8% May forecast. Unemployment reached 4.4% against a 4.2% forecast.

Month in review

All three multi-sector portfolios finished July in positive territory: High Growth returned 0.67%, Growth 0.51% and Moderate Growth 0.36%. Australian Equities was the strongest contributor to absolute returns across the range, adding 2.43% for the month, supported by the energy and financials rotation that lifted the broader market. Property and Infrastructure also added, up 0.56%, while Global Equities detracted modestly at negative 0.43% as the semiconductor unwind weighed on the sleeve's growth and technology exposure, before a strong week of US earnings pared the loss back into month end. Fixed Income was a small drag at negative 0.17%, reflecting the long end selloff that followed the July FOMC meeting. High Growth's larger weighting to Australian and global equities and Moderate Growth's larger weighting to fixed income and cash largely explain the ordering of the three results this month.

Currency remained a live factor for the unhedged global equities exposure. The Australian dollar rose 1.4% against the US dollar to 0.7018 and 0.5% against the euro, reversing June's tailwind and adding a further headwind for offshore holdings translated back to Australian dollars. The MSCI World ex-Australia Index returned -0.92% unhedged in Australian dollars against +0.26% hedged over the month, a gap of 1.2 percentage points that the portfolio's mix of hedged and unhedged global exposure is built to absorb.

July

Market

Twelve months of AI semiconductor leadership unwound in four weeks and then partly rebuilt itself in two sessions. Reports on 28 July that China had begun mass producing deep ultraviolet lithography tools prompted a reassessment of whether Korean and US memory makers can hold their pricing power. The PHLX Semiconductor Index fell 21.0% for the month after roughly doubling across the first half of 2026, and Korea's Kospi fell 22.2% in local currency, its worst month since October 2008, with Samsung Electronics down 21% and SK Hynix down 35% across July. SK Hynix, which listed in the United States on 10 July at US$149, traded below its issue price within three weeks.

US results published in the final week reversed the mood. Microsoft rose 15.5% on 30 July on Azure growth and Amazon 15.3% the following session on cloud, and the read-through to Asian semiconductors was immediate, with the Kospi rising 17.91% on 31 July for its largest single-day gain on record. Japan's Nikkei 225 still finished 8.1% lower in local currency and 7.8% lower in Australian dollars, while the Topix was flat at 0.2%, a divergence that reflects how narrowly the Japanese selloff was concentrated in semiconductor names. The S&P 500 finished 0.1% lower in local currency, Hong Kong rose 13.4% and the FTSE 100 3.6%. Emerging markets carried the damage: MSCI Emerging Markets fell 4.6% and MSCI Asia ex-Japan 4.8% in Australian dollars.

The factor data puts a number on how narrow the unwind was. The MSCI ACWI Momentum Index fell 12.2% in July against 0.1% for the parent index, while High Dividend Yield rose 4.5% and Minimum Volatility 3.6%, a spread of 16.7 percentage points between the best and worst factor in a single month. Quality fell 0.8% and Enhanced Value 3.4%, so the July rotation was out of momentum specifically rather than out of growth as a style.

July

Market

Fed expectations travelled a long way in both directions over the month. Futures began July implying a path to roughly 3.80% by October and close to 4.00% by year end, in effect one and a half further hikes. Oil then did the work: the probability of a September hike rose from below 53% on 16 July to 82% on 23 July as Brent topped US$100, with the odds of a July move rising from under 12% to almost 38% over the same week. The 29 July hold pulled that back, and the month closed with a September hike priced at around 63%.

The curve response was the more revealing part. Markets lowered the odds of near-term tightening while selling the long end: the 30-year rose more than 9 basis points on FOMC day to finish July at 5.25%, its highest since 2007, the 10-year topped 4.7% for the first time since January 2025, and the 2-year fell 4 basis points to 4.24%. That is a repricing of the term premium on inflation credibility rather than of the near-term policy path, and it happened while Warsh dismissed a 0.4% decline in June CPI as not much of a consideration.

Domestic expectations moved the other way. There was no RBA meeting in July and the cash rate remains 4.35%. Cash rate futures implied 60% odds of one final 25 basis point hike to 4.60% by year end as at 20 July, against the RBA's own May Statement assumption of 4.70% by end 2026. The 29 July CPI removed that: the June quarter trimmed mean rose 0.8% for the quarter and 3.6% over the year, below both the market's 0.9% and 3.7% expectations and the RBA's 3.8% profile, while unemployment at 4.4% ran above the RBA's 4.2% June quarter forecast. An August hike is now out of the curve and CommBank expects a hold through the remainder of 2026.

A month in which Korea fell 22.2% and then rallied 17.91% in a single session tells you more about positioning than about earnings. We hold the portfolios to a quality threshold and a valuation discipline that will lag a narrow momentum market and protect capital when the crowding unwinds.
