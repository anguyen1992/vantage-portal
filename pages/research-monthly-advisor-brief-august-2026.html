Key market events in August
1 August
A payrolls contraction moves the next Fed hike from September to December

July non-farm payrolls contracted by 23,000 against consensus for an increase of 80,000, and wage growth moderated to 3.20%, both pointing to a softer US labour market. Markets recalibrated immediately, pushing the next 25 basis point increase from September out to December, and gold rose 7.3% over the week on the combination of lower expected real yields and a softer US dollar. Asian equities were subdued over the same week, with the MSCI Asia ex-Japan Index down around 0.9%, South Korea falling 5.1% and Taiwan gaining 2.5%.

10 to 17 August
Westpac resets the banks and the RBA holds at 4.35%

Westpac opened the bank reporting round on 10 August with home loan applications down 11% over the quarter and around 20% below pre-budget levels, falling 5.9% and taking the S&P/ASX 200 Financials index down 2.2% in morning trade. Commonwealth Bank and NAB each subsequently reported 15% declines in applications, and NAB now forecasts total housing credit growth slowing from about 7.5% to 2.5% in FY27 with investor credit contracting 1.4%. The RBA held the cash rate at 4.35% on 11 August in a unanimous decision, cutting its trimmed mean forecast to 3.3% from 3.5% while lifting its December 2026 unemployment forecast to 4.5% from 4.3%.

20 August
The US long end forces a Treasury intervention

Long-term US borrowing costs reached a near two-decade high before the Treasury announced it would double selected long-dated bond buyback operations to US$4 billion per operation. The measure improved liquidity expectations without addressing the underlying concerns about inflation, debt supply and the federal deficit, and it unsettled currency markets by raising questions about debt management and the dollar's long-term purchasing power. The US dollar index sat at around 98.98 by 24 August, near multi-month lows.

24 August
Gold reaches its highest level since May

Gold futures traded at US$4,706 on 24 August, the highest level since 15 May, extending a weekly gain of more than 5% on a softer dollar, government debt concerns and geopolitical uncertainty. The metal had earlier broken above its 100-day moving average at US$4,387 for the first time in more than two months as US rate hike odds fell. The World Gold Council reported central bank purchases of 288.9 tonnes in the June quarter, up 62% year on year and the strongest second quarter on record. Gold gave back 3.2% in the final week as the dollar recovered, finishing the month at US$4,455 and up 9.7%.

28 August
Warsh turns hawkish at Jackson Hole and puts September back in play

Federal Reserve Chair Kevin Warsh used his first Jackson Hole address to say that while the summer inflation readings had come in better than expected, they do not tell him that underlying trends have meaningfully improved. He recommitted to the 2% PCE target, described the economy as having strengthened, and declined to offer either forward guidance or a reaction function, arguing that market participants should not be looking primarily to the Fed for their next trade. Fed funds futures moved to a 60.4% probability of a quarter point increase in September, up from around 56% before the address, and gold and Asian equities both fell on Monday 31 August as the dollar strengthened.

Macro

What moved markets in August

August was a month of dispersion rather than direction. The S&P/ASX 200 Accumulation Index rose 1.5% for the month, a fifth consecutive gain, but the headline conceals a 27 percentage point spread between healthcare at 18.7% and consumer discretionary at negative 8.1% as FY26 results landed and the banks flagged a housing credit slowdown. Offshore, a US payrolls contraction on 1 August pushed the next Fed hike out to December and lifted gold 9.7%, then Warsh reversed that positioning on 28 August with a hawkish Jackson Hole address that returned September to a 60.4% probability. The Australian dollar rose 2.1% to 0.716 and took 2.2 percentage points out of unhedged global equity returns. Rate hike expectations have since risen on both sides, with the June quarter national accounts on 2 September coming in above expectations and pulling forward domestic pricing.

Equities

ASX 200 Accumulation +1.5% and the MSCI All Countries World Index (AUD) +0.6%. In local currency the S&P 500 rose 3.1%, the Nikkei 225 3.1% and the DAX 2.5%.

Rates

The RBA held at 4.35% on 11 August, unanimously, and decides again on 29 September. September Fed hike odds ran from around 36% after the payrolls contraction to 60.4% after Jackson Hole.

Credit

AusBond Credit fell 0.1% and Credit FRN rose 0.4%. Global Corporate Credit hedged added 0.4% and Global High Yield 0.8% through the long end selloff.

Commodities

Gold rose 9.7% and Brent 7.3% to US$88. The S&P GSCI added 2.4%, Dalian iron ore 5.9% and Newcastle thermal coal 8.9%.

Currency & Policy

The Australian dollar rose 2.1% to 0.716 and 1.3% against the euro, taking the calendar year gain to 7.4%. The RBA cut its trimmed mean forecast to 3.3%.

Key themes

  • Forward guidance set the share price, not the reported result. Fourteen of the 21 held names landed within roughly 2% of consensus on the primary earnings line, yet day one reactions ran from Stockland up 14% to Monadelphous down 11.9%. Monadelphous beat by 2% on both revenue and earnings and delivered a record FY26, then fell because it gave no FY27 guidance on a stretched multiple.
  • The banks confirmed a housing credit slowdown as the May budget tax changes bite. Westpac reported applications around 20% below pre-budget levels, with Commonwealth Bank and NAB both down 15% over the quarter. NAB now forecasts total housing credit growth falling from about 7.5% to 2.5% in FY27, with investor credit contracting 1.4%; ANZ was the exception, with applications broadly flat.
  • Resources delivered on copper leverage and restored dividends. BHP lifted underlying profit 30% to US$13.2 billion, with copper above half of group EBITDA for the first time at a 70% margin, and declared its largest final dividend in four years at US$0.99. Rio Tinto raised the interim dividend 43% to 211 US cents on an 84% rise in copper EBITDA.
  • Quality platforms compounded but had no valuation headroom. HUB24 grew platform funds under administration 24% to $139.5 billion and lifted underlying profit 40%, and still fell 6% on 41 times forward earnings. Goodman guided FY27 earnings per share growth of 9% against the roughly 11% the market carried, and Breville posted record revenue and fell 6% on 30 times.
  • Cost-out and AI productivity became the recurring FY27 margin lever. Commonwealth Bank named AI-driven productivity as its key cost lever while capping investment spend, CSL guided FY27 profit growth of around 5% with most of it from cost reduction, and Woodside set a new US$350 million annual savings target from 2028.
  • Beats did not hold. VanEck put the aggregate S&P/ASX 200 split at 24% beats, 51% in line and 25% misses, close to the Vantage roster's own four beats, 14 in line and three misses across 21 held names. Goldman Sachs strategist Matthew Ross found that almost half the index moved 5% or more on the day of its result, against a long-run average of 28%, and that of the 21 companies which jumped more than 10% on reporting day the average share price has since fallen 4%, with seven underperforming the market by 10%. Ross called the pattern unusual against a long history of beats continuing to outperform by a few per cent over the following one to two months, and attributed the volatility to passive flows, multi-strategy funds and quantitative trading.

Small caps finished August 8.1 percentage points ahead of the S&P/ASX 200, mid caps 4.5 points ahead and equal weight 1.1 points ahead, while large caps lagged by 2.4 points. Consensus has small caps growing forward earnings 26.7% in Y+1 and 25.2% in Y+2, and mid caps 13.8% and 8.4%, against 3.9% and 2.3% for large caps. On price to 12 month forward earnings small caps trade at 19.89 times and large caps at 19.51, so the growth differential is not being paid for at the large cap end, while mid caps at 18.35 times sit below both.

Notable share price reactions

  • Best received among held names were Stockland at plus 14%, with funds from operations of 37 cents at the top of guidance and FY27 guidance around 6% above the consensus midpoint, Origin Energy at plus 6% on adjusted free cash flow up $867 million, and ANZ at plus 4.5% as the strongest of the majors.
  • Softest were Monadelphous at minus 11.9%, nib Holdings at minus 10% on FY27 guidance around 4% below consensus, and ResMed at minus 7.5% on a first ever formal guidance range of 12% to 14% core earnings per share growth. In each case the forward number did the damage.
  • Among the read-through names CSL rose 16%, the largest single move of the round, as underlying profit of US$3.1 billion matched twice-cut May guidance and the feared Vifor outcome did not materialise. Woolworths added 4.9% on Australian food sales up 7.6% in July and August, against Coles at 3.7% across FY26.
Bottom line A two-speed season. Resources and quality compounders beat and re-rated, while banks and priced-for-perfection names were marked down on cautious FY27 guidance rather than on the result itself.

August in review

All three multi-sector portfolios finished August ahead, with High Growth at 1.38%, Growth at 1.19% and Moderate Growth at 0.97%. Australian Equities was the largest contributor to absolute returns at 2.10%, driven by the resources and gold complex as BHP rose 9.82% and Newmont 31.21% through the reporting round. Global Equities added 2.08% and Fixed Income 0.26%, while Property and Infrastructure detracted at negative 2.26% as A-REITs fell 6.7% and global listed infrastructure 2.9%. High Growth's larger weighting to Australian and global equities, and Moderate Growth's larger weighting to fixed income and cash, explain the ordering of the three results.

Financial year to date the same pattern holds, with High Growth at 2.07%, Growth at 1.71% and Moderate Growth at 1.33% across the two months from 1 July. Australian Equities has contributed 4.58% over that period and Global Equities 1.64%, against Property and Infrastructure at negative 1.72%. August month to date

Market

The S&P/ASX 200 Accumulation Index rose 1.5% in August, a fifth consecutive monthly gain, but the sector spread was the widest of the financial year at 27 percentage points. Healthcare led at 18.7% on the CSL relief rally, followed by materials at 12.0% as gold and copper ran, utilities at 7.0% and information technology at 6.3%. Vantage Direct Australian Equities

The portfolio returned 2.10% for August. Newmont led on a percentage basis at 31.21% and Xero at 18.39%, while BHP at 9.82% was the largest single earnings contributor at $117,802, ahead of the SPDR S&P/ASX 200 Fund at 1.42% and $94,143 given its index weight, Newmont at $72,927 and ResMed at 11.18% and $34,448. A round in which fourteen of twenty one held results landed within roughly 2% of consensus and still produced share price reactions from plus 14% to minus 11.9% is a round priced on forward guidance and positioning, not on earnings. That is the environment our process is built for. We hold the portfolios to a quality threshold and a valuation discipline that will look pedestrian when a narrow set of names is being bid up, and that does its work when the market marks a company down for the outlook rather than the result. Monadelphous delivered a record FY26 and a 2% beat on both lines and fell 11.9%; we are buyers of that arithmetic, not sellers of it.
