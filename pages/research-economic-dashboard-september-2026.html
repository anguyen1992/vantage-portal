What changed this month

August 2026
  • Bond yields

    The Australian 10 year rose 17 basis points over August to 5.09% and reached 5.16% on 1 September, the highest level since April 2011, inside a global selloff that took the Japanese 10 year through 3.00% for the first time since 1996 and euro area yields to fresh decade highs. Market pricing moved to 54% for an RBA hike to 4.60% on 29 September from 10% a week earlier, with 40% attached to a subsequent move to 4.85%. Because the United States leg barely moved, the Australia to United States 10 year differential rebounded to roughly 0.38%, and Betashares attributes the global pressure to United States public debt concerns as much as to policy.

  • Policy

    The RBA held at 4.35% on 11 August in a unanimous decision, its second consecutive hold, while repeating that it will raise the cash rate further if upside inflation risks materialise. Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's Jackson Hole address on 28 August was read as hawkish and lifted fed funds futures pricing for a September rise to 60.4%. The Bank of Canada held at 2.25% on 2 September for a seventh consecutive decision, while the RBNZ raised its official cash rate 25 basis points to 2.75%. Markets now carry one full hike by year end in both Australia and the United States, while David Bassanese at Betashares holds a base case of no further move in either this year and reads current yields as reasonable value in fixed rate bonds.

  • Middle East

    The United States and Iran peace deal lapsed during August, though military conflict stayed contained as Washington shifted to economic pressure and Iran and Oman moved towards an agreement on trade flows through the Strait of Hormuz without an explicit deal in place. Oil rose only modestly as a result, with Brent up 0.4% for the month on the Morgan Stanley series and 1.1% on the Betashares reading. The IMF assumes reopening from mid-July with conditions back to their prewar state by March 2027.

  • Inflation

    The July monthly indicator eased to 3.5% year on year from 3.8% in June, while trimmed mean inflation held at 3.6% for a fourth month and remains above the 2% to 3% band. The split among the major banks widened on that print, with NAB moving to forecast a 25 basis point hike in September, ANZ and CBA expecting one in November, and Westpac holding to no change for the rest of 2026.

  • Growth and jobs

    June quarter GDP grew 0.4% and 2.1% through the year, ahead of Morgan Stanley's 0.2% and 1.9%, with household consumption up 0.4%, the saving ratio at 6.5% and the terms of trade down 1.6%. Growth for the 2025-26 financial year was 2.4%. The labour market softened, with unemployment rising to 4.5% in July as employment fell 15,800 and the participation rate dropped 0.2 points to 66.9%.

  • Housing

    Cotality's national index fell 0.9% in August, a fifth consecutive monthly decline, leaving values 3.6% below the March peak, and the July reading was revised from a 0.7% fall to a 1.2% fall. Sydney fell 1.4% and now sits 7.1% below its February peak, 93% of capital city suburbs recorded a decline through winter, and quarterly sales are tracking 15.5% below a year ago with advertised listings 24% higher.

  • Australian equities

    The ASX 200 returned 1.5% for a fifth consecutive monthly gain, reaching a record 9,272 on 6 August before closing the month at 9,076. Materials contributed 302 basis points and Health Care 104, against Financials at minus 184, with BHP adding 110 basis points and CBA subtracting 90. Small Ordinaries returned 5.2% and the MidCap 50 returned 5.8% while the ASX 20 fell 0.6%.

  • Global equities

    Global equities returned 2.7% in local currency but only 0.7% unhedged in Australian dollars, with the 2.1% rise in the currency taking most of the return for an unhedged investor. The global forward multiple rebounded to 17.0 times, still below the 19.6 times reached in October 2025 and at the lower end of its three year range. Confidence in the artificial intelligence thematic was supported by a strong Nvidia result and guidance for further revenue growth.

  • Australian earnings

    FY26 consensus EPS growth lifted to 12.1% as results landed while FY27 was cut 2.0 percentage points to 9.3% and the level of FY27 forecasts fell 1.6%. Across the season 32% of companies beat EPS consensus against 19% missing, and dividends were stronger with 43% ahead and 12% below. Twelve month forward EPS growth has moderated to 8.1%, 4.3 points below its peak.

ElevatedBond yields and the discount rate

The Australian 10 year reached 5.16% on 1 September, its highest since April 2011, with the Japanese 10 year through 3.00% and euro area yields at decade highs. The equity risk premium has compressed to 0.50 percentage points.

ElevatedOil and the Strait of Hormuz

Brent finished August at US$90.50, up 48.7% for the year, after trading above US$100 in late July. The peace deal lapsed during August, though conflict stayed contained as Washington shifted to economic pressure and Iran and Oman moved towards an agreement on Hormuz trade flows. The IMF assumes reopening from mid-July with prewar conditions by March 2027, while noting inventories are near multiyear lows.

ElevatedAustralian housing correction

A fifth consecutive monthly fall has left values 3.6% below the March peak, with 93% of capital city suburbs down through winter and sales tracking 15.5% lower year on year. The reverse wealth effect flows into consumer and bank earnings.

WatchThe RBA hiking again

Markets price 54% for a move to 4.60% on 29 September and 40% for a subsequent move to 4.85%. NAB forecasts a September hike, ANZ and CBA expect November, and Westpac expects no change for the balance of 2026.

WatchBroad commodity inflation

The CRB index is up 37.4% for the year, with wheat, corn and sugar all rising double digits in August alone. The IMF has food prices up 8% and fertiliser up 26% across 2026, which is a second round channel into core inflation.

UpsideThe technology cycle running longer

Consensus has global forward earnings growing 17.3% to the end of 2027, and the S&P 493 delivering 16.6% against 4.9% for the Magnificent Seven, a sharp reversal of recent concentration. Nvidia guided to further revenue growth in August and ISM manufacturing at 55.6 is the strongest reading since May 2022.

Structural: the productivity gap

Ausbil, Jim Chronis · 7 May 2026 · updated twice yearly

Australian productivity has been flat at 0% since 2022 while United States productivity has run at 2% or better for five years, a gap Chronis illustrated as the difference between travelling at 60 and 100 kilometres an hour. Unit labour costs have grown around 5% annually over the past two years against 2% in the United States, which makes the inflation target structurally harder for the RBA to reach and sustains restrictive policy independently of where the cycle sits.

Government spending sits at roughly 28% of GDP with investment at only 5%, maintaining the existing capital stock rather than adding productive capacity, while consumption including the NDIS continues to grow and crowd it out. Ausbil expects GDP to slow from 2.3% to around 1.8% and remain below trend until a private sector led pickup in the second half of 2027, which Chronis characterises as a moderation rather than a contraction. The 2025-26 financial year came in at 2.4%, ahead of that path, and this section is next due for refresh in November.
