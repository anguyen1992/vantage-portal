Key market events in October
15 to 30 October
The AI trade unwinds

Doubts about the return on AI spending hit chip makers, Korean technology shares and AI-linked credit from mid-month. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index fell 15.4% in October and the Nasdaq 5.3%, its worst month since March 2025, with Alphabet down 12.6% in the final week after free cash flow turned negative for a second quarter. Defensive assets did well: global listed infrastructure rose 7.1% (hedged), Australian government bonds 1.3% and global government bonds 1.7% (hedged) as longer-dated yields fell, and Australian utilities 9.3%.

15 and 28 October
Australian inflation re-accelerates as unemployment rises

Headline CPI rose to 4.3% in September and the quarterly trimmed mean rose 1.0%, above the RBA's 0.8% forecast, lifting the annual rate to 3.7%. Unemployment reached 4.7% in September, the highest in five years. The RBA responded on 3 November with a fifth increase this year, to 4.85%.

28 October
The Fed holds and US yields fall back

The Federal Reserve held at 3.75% to 4.00% on a 10 to 2 vote, with Hammack and Cook voting for a rise, and markets priced most of a December increase. The US 10 year yield fell to about 4.92% by 30 October after trading above 5.30% earlier in the month, as investors bought Treasuries during the technology selloff.

Macro

What moved markets in October

October reversed September's pattern. Investors sold the companies most exposed to AI spending, from US and Asian chip makers to Australian data centres, and moved into government bonds and defensive shares. The S&P/ASX 200 rose 1.4% to 8,908, led by utilities (+9.3%) and financials (+7.0%), while the Nasdaq fell 5.3%. At home, September quarter inflation came in above the RBA's forecast, and the RBA raised the cash rate to 4.85% on 3 November.

Equities

S&P/ASX 200 Accumulation +1.4%, closing at 8,908. S&P 500 -0.9% in US dollars and -2.0% in Australian dollars. Nasdaq -5.3%, its worst month since March 2025.

Rates

The Fed held at 3.75% to 4.00%. The US 10 year fell about 36 basis points to 4.92%. The RBA raised to 4.85% on 3 November.

Earnings

With 60% of S&P 500 companies reported, 82% beat on earnings, but by an average of only 1.9%, the smallest margin since late 2022.

Commodities

Brent rose 13.6% to US$117.6 a barrel as Middle East supply stayed disrupted. Gold fell 6.8% to US$3,875 and iron ore 3.2% to US$91.1.

Currency & Policy

The Australian dollar rose 1.2% to US 70.3 cents, the strongest G10 currency for the month, which reduced unhedged offshore returns. Headline CPI rose to 4.3% in September.

3 November: the RBA raised the cash rate 25 basis points to 4.85%. The decision was carried six to three and is the fifth increase this year, taking the cash rate to its highest level in 18 years. Markets had priced an 88% chance of the move after the September quarter trimmed mean of 1.0% came in above the Bank's forecast, and Deputy Governor Hauser had said on 21 October that the inflation outlook was materially worse than projected in August. Markets now price about an even chance of a further increase taking the cash rate above 5%. The Statement on Monetary Policy released the same day carries the RBA's new forecasts.
Market rotation

The rotation out of AI

About US$3.46 trillion of market value was wiped from AI-linked stocks in October, as investors questioned whether the spending on chips, data centres and memory will earn an adequate return. The largest falls came in memory and storage and AI cloud providers.

A narrow sell-off

  • Semiconductors led the falls. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index fell 15.4% in October, from 12,629 to 10,689, including an eight session losing run to 16 October, its longest since 2008, and a 7.2% fall on 30 October alone. From its October high of 13,350 the index fell as much as 21.8%. Investors questioned whether AI infrastructure spending will earn an adequate return, and a higher than expected US inflation reading added to the pressure.
  • Asian AI stocks fell further than the US. TSMC fell 11.0% and the US-listed shares of SK hynix 19.9% over the month. The KOSPI fell 16.2% in local currency for the month, and MSCI Emerging Markets 9.1% in Australian dollars.
  • The sell-off was narrow and concentrated in technology. The Kobeissi Letter estimates the Nasdaq 100 lost about US$4 trillion of value in 25 days, while its "S&P 500 excluding AI" measure, which removes technology and communication services, closed at a record high on 30 October.
  • The concern is the return on capital spending. AI-related stocks make up about 48% of the S&P 500, with Nvidia alone about 7.5%, and AI-related corporate bond issuance has reached a record US$419 billion this year as the build-out is increasingly funded with debt and new equity. Kobeissi calls this a "stealth" bear market inside a bull market and expects the correction to prove healthy.
  • Australian AI infrastructure repriced. Firmus, the Nvidia-backed AI data centre developer, withdrew its ASX float on 9 October after super funds and fund managers would not support an $11 offer price that valued it at about A$44 billion, against A$1.85 billion in September 2025. NextDC fell 41.7% on 21 October, its worst day since listing in 2010, after delays to Jemena's power upgrades at its M3 Melbourne site deferred about A$140 million of FY27 contracted revenue. The company cut FY27 revenue guidance to A$475 million to A$500 million, from A$615 million to A$640 million.
  • Credit spreads on AI-linked borrowers widened. CoreWeave's five-year credit default swap spread rose above 1,100 basis points and its 2031 bonds yielded as much as 16% in mid-October, according to S&P Global Market Intelligence data reported by Reuters. Oracle's five-year spread rose about 31 basis points in a week to near 300, and its shares fell 8.8% on 15 October. The cost of default protection on Amazon, Alphabet and Meta also reached record highs, although it rose more slowly.

Money moved back into defensive assets. US Treasuries rallied as equities fell, with the 10 year yield finishing the month near 4.92% from above 5.30%.

October was a timely reminder of the value of staying diversified away from the AI theme that has driven markets over the past 12 to 24 months. The defensive parts of our portfolios did their job. Macquarie Dynamic Bond (+1.66%) and Bentham Global Income (+1.64%) gained as bond yields fell, and the Property and Infrastructure portfolio returned 5.16%, led by the VanEck infrastructure ETF (+8.74%), Ausbil Global Essential Infrastructure (+8.20%) and ATLAS (+6.67%). In global equities, Aoris International (+7.77%), which invests in a concentrated portfolio of high-quality businesses, was our strongest manager.

Global equities

US reporting season and the Magnificent 7

Reporting season so far

  • Most companies beat, by less. With 60% of the S&P 500 reported, 82% beat earnings estimates against a five-year average of 78%. The average beat was 1.9%, against 7.5% normally and the smallest since late 2022.
  • Growth remains strong. Blended earnings growth for the September quarter is 30.2% and revenue growth 13.4%, with analysts expecting 28.4% earnings growth in the December quarter.
  • Valuations eased. The forward P/E fell to 18.7 times from 19.0 at the end of September, below its five-year average of 19.8.

Global Equities portfolio positioning at 30 September

  • Underweight the Magnificent 7 by 9.4 points (15.8% against 25.2%), mainly through NVIDIA and Apple. Microsoft is the only one held above index weight.
  • Underweight North America at 64.0% against 77.7%, with the difference held in emerging markets (+9.4 points) and the UK (+3.0 points).
  • Held 3.1% in Asian chip and memory makers (TSMC 0.84%, Kioxia 0.81%, Samsung 0.77%, SK Hynix 0.66%) against 0.15% in VGS, mainly through the emerging markets overweight. These were among the names hit hardest in the mid-October selloff.
  • Overweight financials (+2.1 points) and technology (+1.2 points), the latter through holdings outside the Magnificent 7. Underweight communication services (-3.2 points), consumer staples (-1.8 points) and utilities (-1.3 points).

October in review

  • All three portfolios rose and finished ahead of their objectives. Moderate Growth returned 0.84%, Growth 0.93% and High Growth 1.03%, against CPI objectives of 0.46% to 0.58%. High Growth gained most because it holds the most Australian equities and real assets, which were the two strongest sleeves.
  • Property and Infrastructure +5.16%. Infrastructure led as bond yields fell and investors moved into defensive assets, with the VanEck infrastructure ETF up 8.74%, Ausbil Global Essential Infrastructure 8.20% and ATLAS 6.67%. Property lagged, with Quay Global Real Estate up 2.95% and the VanEck international property ETF 2.53%.
  • Australian Equities +2.14%. Utilities and financials led the market as investors moved out of resources. Origin Energy (+10.60%), AGL (+9.83%), Commonwealth Bank (+9.67%) and QBE (+11.33%) rose, while Rio Tinto (-10.95%) and BHP (-4.83%) fell with iron ore and Newmont (-6.42%) with gold.
  • Global Equities -0.79%. The portfolio fell less than MSCI World ex Australia (-1.94% in Australian dollars). Aoris (+7.77%) offset most of the fall in T. Rowe Price Global Equity (-5.82%), the most growth-oriented manager, which fell with US technology.
  • Fixed Interest +0.50%. Macquarie Dynamic Bond (+1.66%) and Bentham Global Income (+1.64%) gained as yields fell.
